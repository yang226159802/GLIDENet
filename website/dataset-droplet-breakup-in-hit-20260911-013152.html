Droplet breakup in HIT

Direct numerical simulation data of a liquid droplet breaking up in forced homogeneous isotropic turbulence at Weber number 15

On this page

Description Dataset Access Quick Info

Description

The DNS is performed using a mass-conserving level-set method. The incompressible Navier–Stokes equations are solved on a staggered uniform grid with a second-order finite central-difference scheme and a projection method. A second-order semi-implicit iterative procedure is used for time integration, and the ghost-fluid method is used for the pressure jump and viscous terms. The level-set and re-initialization equations are discretized with a fifth-order upstream central scheme and integrated with a second-order semi-implicit Crank–Nicolson scheme. The interface is represented by the zero iso-surface of the level-set function; liquid is ϕ>0, carrier is ϕ<0, and the interface is ϕ=0. The fast marching method and a least-squares method are used for curvature reconstruction.
